The students

沈文Shěn Wén— the builder —

The easygoing American strangers keep addressing in quick Chinese — he reads local until he answers. He builds: robotics, AI, whatever’s broken on the table in front of him. He says the least of anyone there and is the reason there’s a table; his handmade robot, 小圖, rides on his bag strap wherever he goes.

這個可以修。Zhège kěyǐ xiū.“This can be fixed.”

阮草Ruǎn Cǎo— the Tender —

Things live for her. She grows mushrooms and runs ferments off a shelf in the corner of the café, and the group borrowed her word for it — mát tay, the cool hand. Headstrong, direct, and warmly certain she’s right, she shows love by doing: the right mushroom pulled for the right person on the right day.

活的東西急不來。Huó de dōngxi jí bù lái.“Living things can’t be hurried.”

星野遙香Xīngyě Yáoxiāng— the super-fan —

The Osaka super-fan who’s more into Taiwan than the locals are — loud, quick, and always behind her film camera, documenting everything. She’s the group’s energy, the half-step-ahead one who gets everyone out the door: first to try the stinky tofu, last to want the night to end.

超級好看的啦！Chāojí hǎokàn de la!“It’s so good-looking!”

金多恩Jīn Duō’ēn— the conservator —

Quiet, sharp, unhurried — the group’s best test-taker, who says the least and notices the most. She lands the plain hard verdict in a handful of cold words, then quietly picks up the broken thing everyone else stepped over and mends it.

看得出來，才是好的。Kàn de chūlái, cái shì hǎo de.“That you can see it is what makes it good.”

阿迪Ā Dí— the maker / the fashionable one —

The warm, laid-back maker from Jakarta who stitches batik into streetwear and plans the whole day around the next meal — the one who ends an argument by getting everyone to sit down and eat. What the shirts earn goes home to his family; he carries that quietly, and he is the last one in the room to decide anything.

不要緊張，又不會怎樣。Bùyào jǐnzhāng, yòu bù huì zěnyàng.“Don’t stress — nothing’s going to happen.”

白龍Bái Lóng— the deadpan far-seer —

A towering, bone-dry Englishman with a cavernous bass and binoculars round his neck — he says the least in any room and sees the furthest, calling the rain or the crowd a beat before it lands, then saying nothing about having been right. The café’s ginger tom 將軍, who likes nobody, sleeps across his shoulder.

The only local in the group, and the only one with tools — practical, quick, a little cheeky. She knows the right stall and the right bus, ducks into a temple to 拜拜 between errands, and will have your scooter running before you’ve finished explaining what it’s doing.

Faculty & the café

蘇老師Sū Lǎoshī— the name-giver —

Their warm, unhurried teacher, who has your name by the end of day one and hands you the Chinese one you’ll answer to from then on — 白龍’s too-grand name is hers, and so is 小圖’s. Then she asks the question you’d been steering around, and waits.

你先想，我不急。Nǐ xiān xiǎng, wǒ bù jí.“You think first — I’m not in a hurry.”

吳老闆Wú Lǎobǎn— the host —

The gruff-but-soft host of 珍貓 — the cat café that also pours bubble tea, and the group’s living room. He knows the students have no money, so the cups come back a little too full and a red-bean bun turns up that nobody ordered — then he swears the extra scoop was an accident.

老規矩，珍奶一杯！Lǎo guīju, zhēnnǎi yì bēi!“The usual — one bubble tea!”

The café’s own

小圖Xiǎo Tú— the handmade companion —

沈文’s keychain-sized handmade robot — dreamy, soft-spoken, always a half-step inside his own world. He rides on 沈文’s bag strap to class and sits on the desk through the whole lesson, listening: lit sprout-green eyes for a whole face, and more heart than a palm-sized toy has any right to.

我剛剛在想一件事情。Wǒ gānggāng zài xiǎng yī jiàn shìqing.“I was just thinking about something.”

將軍General— the café cat, 白龍’s first friend —

The café’s resident ginger tom — 將軍不喜歡人, he doesn’t like people, and naps where he pleases. He long ago chose exactly one person, 白龍, for reasons nobody — 白龍 included — can explain.
