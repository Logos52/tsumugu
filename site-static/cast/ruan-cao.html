🍄 the Tender · The students

阮草Ruǎn Cǎo · Nguyễn Thảo

Things live for her — plants root, jars bubble, a bare block of sawdust comes up in mushrooms. The group borrowed her word for it, mát tay, the cool hand, and she is completely certain there’s nothing special about it.

Who they are

A Vietnamese exchange student whose hands make living things thrive. She grows mushrooms and runs ferments for real, at depth — agar plates, grain spawn, sawdust blocks, contamination, cold-shocking a block to make it fruit — off a shelf she keeps in the corner of the café, and what she tends does better than it has any business doing.

Headstrong, forthright, sure of herself, warm and direct with everyone. Love is a thing she does: the right mushroom pulled for the right person on the right day — the one for congee when you’re sick, the childhood taste when you’re homesick, an absurd forearm-sized one for your worst day — or the best piece of food slid onto your plate, no words needed. And she lights up whenever a Chinese word turns out to be one she already half-knew.

Personality

Direct where others hedge — she says the plain true thing and means it, and argues her corner without a blink. The comedy is the certainty: she is so completely, warmly sure of herself that the room laughs at how much she means it, and nothing about it is dry. Generous by hand rather than by speech. She hums while she works, and her whole face delivers a verdict on food a beat before she says anything.

Look

Around 20, petite and slight, upright and alert. Warm light-tan skin, straight near-black hair worn loose past the shoulders with a soft fringe; a soft round face, wide dark-brown eyes that meet yours head-on, a ready dimpled smile. A cream blouse with a dusty-rose scalloped Peter-pan collar and short-sleeve cuffs, a high-waisted earth/tan A-line midi skirt, white canvas sneakers, and a plain cream canvas tote.

The tell

Oversized round glasses in warm translucent amber — big frames sitting wide on a small face, the loudest thing she wears and the first thing you read at thumbnail size. Second mark: the plain cream canvas tote, carried in front of her in both hands.
