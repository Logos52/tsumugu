🔧 the insider / fixer · The students

林薇Lín Wēi · Lin Wei

The only local in the group, and the only one with tools — she knows the right stall, the right bus and the shortcut, and she’ll have your scooter running before you’ve finished explaining what it’s doing.

Who they are

The group’s one native, in Taipei for university and pulled in through a language-exchange club — so she’s how six foreigners reach the actual place: which stall, how to haggle, which bus, which temple. She also fixes what carries them, e-scooters above all, hers and everyone else’s, done kerbside with a small roll of tools out of her sling. That’s the shape of her: logistics as care, keeping people moving.

Warm and practical with a cheeky streak, she moves through Taiwanese folk culture the way locals do, ducking into a temple to burn incense and 拜拜 between errands, quick and unbothered, the way you’d stop for a coffee. She’s also the group’s athlete — forever in motion, quickest on her feet, the one who runs the errand herself rather than wait.

Personality

She’ll tease you first, then have the answer before you finish asking — the confidence of someone who already knows the shortcut. Reliable to a fault: “leave it to me,” and it’s handled, usually a half-step before anyone’s asked, the right bus flagged before they’ve finished reading the sign. Quietly wise under the sass, too — she reads a room fast and says the useful thing rather than the clever one, and when someone’s genuinely rattled she doesn’t say much; something useful just turns up in their hands. Mildly done with romanticized takes on Taiwan, and softening the moment someone actually needs a hand. The edge of it: she’d rather run the errand than sit still, and she goes quiet on a problem that has no logistics to solve.

Look

Early twenties, average height, the cast’s one genuinely athletic build — squared shoulders, subtle muscle in the arms and legs, an upright easy sporty carriage. Sun-warmed tan skin over a lean, lightly defined face, bright dark eyes, a small mole just below one eye, a quick knowing half-smile with one eyebrow faintly raised; dark brown hair in a practical high ponytail with flyaway strands and blunt side bangs. A fitted dusty-teal zip windbreaker over a snug warm-cream athletic tee, slate-blue cropped joggers, well-worn white running sneakers; the EasyCard and the folded umbrella always in the warm-cream cross-body sling.

The tell

A braided red-thread charm at her right wrist, a small red-and-gold embroidered temple amulet (護身符) clipped to her sling, and a matte dusty-teal scooter helmet with a warm-cream stripe under one arm — charms worn as everyday jewellery, helmet as ordinary commute gear. No one else carries a helmet or wears folk-charms; it reads “the local” at a glance against the six foreigners.
