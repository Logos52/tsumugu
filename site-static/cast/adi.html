🧥 the maker / the odds-man · The students

阿迪Ā Dí · Adi

The host who stitches batik into streetwear and plans the whole day around the next meal — then turns into a completely different animal the second anything has a score attached.

Who they are

A design student from Jakarta with a small streetwear side-hustle: he fuses Indonesian batik with modern cuts and turns out custom shirts for the whole group. He’s how six people end up in the same room — he knows the stall, the noodle, the hour the bao come out hot, and his answer to most problems is sit, eat, we’re family. Generous with food, with shirts, with his time, and easily pulled off-task by a good smell coming off a grill.

He also never stops running numbers. He prices anything at a glance, always has a drop or a trade going, and inside a game with points he is merciless — claw machines, Mario Kart, who-spots-the-bus-first — then chill again a second later like none of it happened. The running rivalry with 金多恩 has yet to score him a single win.

Personality

Warm, easy, hard to rattle — the one who lowers the temperature in a room, usually with a joke at his own expense or a plate slid in front of whoever’s upset. He’d rather feed a disagreement than have one, so he’s rarely the one who says the hard thing out loud, and he’ll duck a direct question with food, a game, or a laugh. Then something gets a score on it and the whole register flips: locked in, competitive, talking trash — and back to unbothered the instant it’s over. The switch is the joke, and the group never gets tired of it. The chill is how he plays it, with the drive running underneath the whole time.

Look

Early twenties, medium height, an ordinary average build carried in a relaxed, unhurried way — at most the faintest softness at the middle. Warm tan-brown skin, an easygoing open face with regular cheeks and an ordinary jawline, a broad easy smile, wide nose, thick dark brows, heavy-lidded dark-brown eyes, a day or two of light stubble. Thick black wavy hair grown out on top and swept back, a bit unruly, shorter at the sides; a small silver ear stud; faint fabric-marker smudges on the backs of his hands from screen-printing his own shirts.

The tell

The self-made batik bomber — dense soga-brown-and-cream kawung/parang panels across chest and sleeves, reading before you reach his face — worn open, with slim low-profile on-ear headphones in soga-brown and black round his neck. He’s the only one in the cast who wears pattern; the batik and the neck-headphones fix his silhouette.
